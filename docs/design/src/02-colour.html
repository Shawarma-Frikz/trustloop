Every band appears with its score, its name, and its position (“Band 2 of 6”) in the panel and in the accessible name. The measure itself is a position cue. A colour-blind reader must be able to identify the band without seeing the hue.

SECTION 03 · APPLICATIONHow much colour

The band colour occupies about two percent of the panel. The rest is paper, ink, and rule. This is why the estimate reads as evidence rather than as a traffic light.

3.3 Surface ratio

70% paper and ink 20% neutral rules and surfaces 8% petrol 2% band colour

The ratio applies to in-product surfaces, not to the cover or a deliberate brand page. In the panel, band colour appears in three places only: the chip's left rule, the measure segments, and the marker's band label. It never becomes a full-width background for a review row.

3.5 Colour rules

  • No gradients. Anywhere in the product, including the cover of this charter.
  • No pure black or white. Ink and paper carry the contrast.
  • No red/green traffic lights. Rust and pine mean direction, not “bad” and “good”.
  • No colour-only encoding. Label, numeral, and position always accompany hue.
  • No tints of a band. A 20% tint cannot stand in for the band; use the exact stop or the neutral rule.
  • No band colour on a page aggregate. There is no page aggregate.
  • No colour on body text. Links and actions use petrol; emphasis uses weight, not hue.
  • No decorative shadow. The hard 3 px panel offset is a print device, not elevation.

SECTION 03 · NIGHTThe night ledger

A dark host page should not force a dark panel, but readers who prefer dark interfaces need one. The night theme is a separate ledger, not an inverted screenshot: the paper becomes ink, the rules stay visible, and the six bands are re-measured for dark contrast.
