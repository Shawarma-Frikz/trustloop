SECTION 01The idea

TrustLoop does not tell you whether a review is true. It gives the text a second reading, shows which patterns moved the estimate, and says plainly when it cannot estimate at all.

What it does

Estimates whether review text reads as organic or manipulated, using text patterns only. Returns a score, a band, evidence quality, up to five signals, and the model version.

What it refuses

No identity, purchase, or truth claim. No page average. No guess when the text, language, or service cannot support one. Unknown is a designed outcome, not a failure to hide.

Why the loop is open

A closed loop is a seal of approval. TrustLoop's mark stays open: the reading returns to the text, and the missing segment is where doubt lives.

1.3 What TrustLoop is not

Not a fraud detector

It estimates a text pattern. It cannot prove intent, identity, purchase, or truth. The interface never uses “fraud”, “fake”, “bot account”, or “verified”.

Not a page score

Reviews stay individual. There is no average, no product grade, no “trustworthy seller” badge. Counts of reviews are allowed; an aggregate verdict is not.

Not a surveillance tool

No reviewer names, avatars, profiles, or identity cues appear in the panel. The interface reads the text, not the person.

Not an AI product page

No sparkles, robots, neural-network illustrations, or “powered by AI” labels. The method is named in the details, in plain language, where it belongs.

SECTION 01 · MARKThe open loop

The mark is a circle with a missing segment and a small square resting in the gap. The square is the review; the opening is the limit of what the system can know.

Construction · 24-unit grid

Geometry

  • Outer diameter: 17 units of a 24-unit box; stroke 2 units.
  • The gap spans 80°, centred on the horizontal axis.
  • The square is 2.5 units, aligned to the gap centre, with equal space above and below.
  • Terminals are square, not rounded. The mark should feel cut, not drawn with a pen.

Clear space

Keep at least half the mark's height clear on every side. In the horizontal lockup, the space between mark and wordmark is 0.6× the mark height.

Minimum size

Mark: 16 px on screen, 6 mm in print. Horizontal lockup: 88 px wide. Below that, use the mark alone.

1.5 Misuse

Do not close the loop

A closed circle reads as a seal of approval or a completed verification. TrustLoop never claims either.

Do not add a check, shield, or eye

These borrow the visual language of security and surveillance. The product is neither.

Do not rotate, outline, or recolour

Use petrol on paper, paper on petrol. No gradients, no drop shadow, no decorative animation.

The misuse test

If a change makes the mark look more official, more certain, or more like a security product, it is wrong — even when it looks better. The loop stays open, the palette stays earth, and the square never moves out of the gap.

SECTION 02Principles

Six rules decide most of the interface. When a new screen is ambiguous, apply them in order. The first rule wins.

01

The number is never alone

A score always appears with its band, its evidence quality, and its engine. Never a naked “73”, never a number without the sentence that limits it.

In practice: the chip may show only the score because the panel is one click away; the panel always shows the full cluster.

02

Doubt is a state, not an error

Unknown, low evidence, signals-only, timeout: each has a designed layout, plain copy, and a next action. None is a red error toast.

In practice: the unknown chip is hollow, not red; the panel explains the reason in one sentence.

03

Evidence before decoration

Every line, bar, and mark carries meaning. No ornamental charts, no decorative gradients, no icons that repeat the label.

In practice: if a visual element can be removed without losing information, remove it.

04

Paper, not glass

Flat surfaces, hairline rules, solid ink. The interface should feel printed and inspectable, not floating and translucent.

In practice: a 1 px rule and a hard 3 px offset replace blur, glow, and elevation.

05

Read the text, not the person

No avatars, names, profiles, flags, or identity cues. Reviews are identified by their order on the page, not by who wrote them.

In practice: “Review 3”, not a username or a profile picture.

06

Two languages, one claim

French and English carry the same strength of claim. A longer French sentence may wrap; it may not become more certain or more vague.

In practice: design at the longer string and test both at 320 px.

Decision test · use before adding a visual

1. Does it clarify a state, a direction, or a limit? 2. Would it survive without colour? 3. Does it imply more certainty, identity, or aggregation than the model has? 4. Would a careful reader mistake it for a verdict? If any answer is wrong, the element does not ship.

SECTION 02 · REFUSALSThe tells we refuse

“AI-made” is rarely one bad colour. It is a cluster of shortcuts that signal a template rather than a point of view. These are wrong here for a specific reason, not because they are universally bad.

Violet or indigo gradients

Generic SaaS branding. They also imply a polished intelligence the model does not have. Use paper, petrol, rust, and the six stops.

Glassmorphism and blur

Frosted cards look like an AI dashboard and reduce text contrast over a busy host page. Surfaces are opaque.

Sparkles, robots, brains

They advertise a technology instead of explaining an estimate. The panel names the model version in the footer, quietly.

Shields, checks, seals

They claim safety and verification. TrustLoop estimates text patterns and says so. A checkmark is never the verdict icon.

Gauges, needles, rings

They make a probability look like a physical measurement. The measure is a banded rule, not a dial.

Emoji and emoji-like icons

They set an informal, chat-app tone and render differently across platforms. Icons are drawn, 1.5 px, single colour.

Avatars and reviewer cards

They invite judgments about a person. Review rows are numbered. Identity is out of scope.

Centered everything

Centered marketing layouts are hard to scan in a 360 px panel. The panel is left-aligned and rule-led.

The replacement rule

When a template suggests a familiar AI visual, replace it with a documentary one: a rule, a numbered row, a stamped label, a measured band, or a plain sentence. The interface should look like it was made by someone who read the reviews.
