SECTION 01The idea

TrustLoop does not tell you whether a review is true. It gives the text a second reading, shows which patterns moved the estimate, and says plainly when it cannot estimate at all.

What it does

Estimates whether review text reads as organic or manipulated, using text patterns only. Returns a score, a band, evidence quality, up to five signals, and the model version.

What it refuses

No identity, purchase, or truth claim. No page average. No guess when the text, language, or service cannot support one. Unknown is a designed outcome, not a failure to hide.

Why the loop is open

A closed loop is a seal of approval. TrustLoop's mark stays open: the reading returns to the text, and the missing segment is where doubt lives.

1.3 What TrustLoop is not

Not a fraud detector

It estimates a text pattern. It cannot prove intent, identity, purchase, or truth. The interface never uses “fraud”, “fake”, “bot account”, or “verified”.

Not a page score

Reviews stay individual. There is no average, no product grade, no “trustworthy seller” badge. Counts of reviews are allowed; an aggregate verdict is not.

Not a surveillance tool

No reviewer names, avatars, profiles, or identity cues appear in the panel. The interface reads the text, not the person.

Not an AI product page

No sparkles, robots, neural-network illustrations, or “powered by AI” labels. The method is named in the details, in plain language, where it belongs.

SECTION 01 · MARKThe open loop

The mark is a circle with a missing segment and a small square resting in the gap. The square is the review; the opening is the limit of what the system can know.

Construction · 24-unit grid

Geometry

  • Outer diameter: 17 units of a 24-unit box; stroke 2 units.
  • The gap spans 80°, centred on the horizontal axis.
  • The square is 2.5 units, aligned to the gap centre, with equal space above and below.
  • Terminals are square, not rounded. The mark should feel cut, not drawn with a pen.

Clear space

Keep at least half the mark's height clear on every side. In the horizontal lockup, the space between mark and wordmark is 0.6× the mark height.

Minimum size

Mark: 16 px on screen, 6 mm in print. Horizontal lockup: 88 px wide. Below that, use the mark alone.

1.5 Misuse

Do not close the loop

A closed circle reads as a seal of approval or a completed verification. TrustLoop never claims either.

Do not add a check, shield, or eye

These borrow the visual language of security and surveillance. The product is neither.

Do not rotate, outline, or recolour

Use petrol on paper, paper on petrol. No gradients, no drop shadow, no decorative animation.

The misuse test

If a change makes the mark look more official, more certain, or more like a security product, it is wrong — even when it looks better. The loop stays open, the palette stays earth, and the square never moves out of the gap.

SECTION 02Principles

Six rules decide most of the interface. When a new screen is ambiguous, apply them in order. The first rule wins.

01

The number is never alone

A score always appears with its band, its evidence quality, and its engine. Never a naked “73”, never a number without the sentence that limits it.

In practice: the chip may show only the score because the panel is one click away; the panel always shows the full cluster.

02

Doubt is a state, not an error

Unknown, low evidence, signals-only, timeout: each has a designed layout, plain copy, and a next action. None is a red error toast.

In practice: the unknown chip is hollow, not red; the panel explains the reason in one sentence.

03

Evidence before decoration

Every line, bar, and mark carries meaning. No ornamental charts, no decorative gradients, no icons that repeat the label.

In practice: if a visual element can be removed without losing information, remove it.

04

Paper, not glass

Flat surfaces, hairline rules, solid ink. The interface should feel printed and inspectable, not floating and translucent.

In practice: a 1 px rule and a hard 3 px offset replace blur, glow, and elevation.

05

Read the text, not the person

No avatars, names, profiles, flags, or identity cues. Reviews are identified by their order on the page, not by who wrote them.

In practice: “Review 3”, not a username or a profile picture.

06

Two languages, one claim

French and English carry the same strength of claim. A longer French sentence may wrap; it may not become more certain or more vague.

In practice: design at the longer string and test both at 320 px.

Decision test · use before adding a visual

1. Does it clarify a state, a direction, or a limit? 2. Would it survive without colour? 3. Does it imply more certainty, identity, or aggregation than the model has? 4. Would a careful reader mistake it for a verdict? If any answer is wrong, the element does not ship.

SECTION 02 · REFUSALSThe tells we refuse

“AI-made” is rarely one bad colour. It is a cluster of shortcuts that signal a template rather than a point of view. These are wrong here for a specific reason, not because they are universally bad.

Violet or indigo gradients

Generic SaaS branding. They also imply a polished intelligence the model does not have. Use paper, petrol, rust, and the six stops.

Glassmorphism and blur

Frosted cards look like an AI dashboard and reduce text contrast over a busy host page. Surfaces are opaque.

Sparkles, robots, brains

They advertise a technology instead of explaining an estimate. The panel names the model version in the footer, quietly.

Shields, checks, seals

They claim safety and verification. TrustLoop estimates text patterns and says so. A checkmark is never the verdict icon.

Gauges, needles, rings

They make a probability look like a physical measurement. The measure is a banded rule, not a dial.

Emoji and emoji-like icons

They set an informal, chat-app tone and render differently across platforms. Icons are drawn, 1.5 px, single colour.

Avatars and reviewer cards

They invite judgments about a person. Review rows are numbered. Identity is out of scope.

Centered everything

Centered marketing layouts are hard to scan in a 360 px panel. The panel is left-aligned and rule-led.

3.5 Colour rules

  • No gradients. Anywhere in the product, including the cover of this charter.
  • No pure black or white. Ink and paper carry the contrast.
  • No red/green traffic lights. Rust and pine mean direction, not “bad” and “good”.
  • No colour-only encoding. Label, numeral, and position always accompany hue.
  • No tints of a band. A 20% tint cannot stand in for the band; use the exact stop or the neutral rule.
  • No band colour on a page aggregate. There is no page aggregate.
  • No colour on body text. Links and actions use petrol; emphasis uses weight, not hue.
  • No decorative shadow. The hard 3 px panel offset is a print device, not elevation.

SECTION 06The measure

A printed scale, not a gauge. The measure states a position on a bounded scale, names the band that position falls in, and shows the evidence that permits the claim. When there is no estimate, it shows no position at all.

6.1 Why a rule, not a dial

A dial implies a target and a safe zone. A needle implies a live reading of a physical quantity. A ring implies completeness: at 100% the ring closes and the task is done. None of those metaphors belong to a text estimate that is bounded, fallible, and never a verdict.

The banded rule comes from printed reports: a bounded axis, divided into named intervals, with a mark at the measured value. It is precise without being mechanical. It can be read at a glance, read again with a screen reader, and understood without colour.

One sentence

The measure never fills. A fill implies progress toward a goal. Trust is not a goal.

The six stops are always present, always solid, and always in the same order. Only the marker moves.

Refused

No speedometer arc, no needle, no percentage ring, no traffic light, no star rating, no single-colour progress bar, no animated sweep from zero. Each of them adds certainty the model does not have.

6.6 Motion and behaviour

  • The marker appears at its value. It never sweeps in from zero, and the score never counts up. Counting up stages certainty as an event.
  • Rescan crossfades. 120 ms opacity on the measure block; no slide, no rotation, no width animation.
  • No fill animation. The stops are static. Only the marker's left position changes, and only between two scored values.

  • No number formatting. No commas, no decimals, no “out of 100”. The scale labels already say 100.
  • The accessible name carries the whole measure. Score, band, evidence, and direction of the marker are announced together; the six stops are not read as six separate items.

SECTION 08States and integration

The honest cases are the design. A missing estimate, a short review, an unreachable service, and a page with no reviews are not edge cases to be hidden; they are the moments when the product either keeps its promise or breaks it.

8.1 A failure is a sentence

Every failed or absent estimate resolves to one of three shapes: a sentence, a reason, and — only where it can help — one action. No infinite spinner, no red panel, no alarm icon, no “Oops”, no mascot, no apology paragraph.

The tone is procedural. The page is not broken; the estimate is absent. Say which, say why, and stop. A failure never borrows the visual language of a verdict: no band colour, no measure, no score cell.

Never

  • Show 0 for an unknown value.
  • Show a spinner after the request has failed.
  • Show a raw reason code in the interface.
  • Offer “Retry” for a page that will never be supported.
  • Keep a chip that no longer matches the page.

SECTION 09Language and content

The interface speaks about text patterns with the caution of a lab note and the brevity of a label. It never borrows the vocabulary of verification, and it never says in one language more than it says in the other.

Plain

11.3 Reduced motion

Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, every duration collapses to 0 ms: expansion is instant, the crossfade is a swap, and the skeleton pulse stops. Nothing is lost: the content was never carried by the motion. The same rule applies to forced-colors mode, where the browser replaces the palette and the interface must remain legible through system colours.

No performance

The score does not animate because the model is not “thinking” at the user. The estimate arrives; the interface shows it.

No suspense

No delayed reveal, no staged signals, no “analysing…” sequence of fake steps. A loading line and a skeleton are enough.

No attention loop

Nothing pulses after load. The only repeating motion is the skeleton pulse while a request is genuinely pending.

11.4 Implementation notes

  • Animate opacity and transform only, except the row height needed for expansion. No animated box-shadow, filter, or background-position.
  • Respect the reduced-motion query in both CSS and script; do not rely on the media query alone if a script drives the expansion.
  • Keep the list scroll position when a row expands, and never animate the scroll itself.
  • Pause the skeleton pulse when the panel is not visible; a hidden tab should not animate.

12.6 Token rules

  • No raw values in components. A hex code, px padding, or duration outside this file is a defect. The review greps for # and px in component styles.
  • No gradients. There is no gradient token because no product surface uses one. A gradient in a mockup is a mistake, not a theme.
  • No colour-only states. A token cannot add a cue that the markup does not carry. The band ladder, arrow, label, and em dash exist in the component, not in the palette.
  • Night is explicit. The theme follows the extension setting or the OS preference, never the host page. It is applied with data-tl-theme="night", not by inverting values.
  • One new token needs a decision. Add the value to both files, update this section, and record it in D20. Do not sneak a one-off value into a component.
  • Values are versioned. A colour change is a charter revision, not a tweak. The PDF, the CSS, the JSON, and the Figma variables carry the same version.
The 4 px trap

Ten, fourteen, eighteen, and twenty-two are not spacing tokens. The 14 px mockup padding from an early draft was corrected to the 16 px gutter; the only off-scale offset is the 3 px hard shadow, which is a print device, not spacing.

15.6 Questions to expect

QuestionAnswer
“Is this an AI that detects fake reviews?”No. It estimates whether text patterns resemble manipulation. “Fake” is a claim about intent and truth that the model cannot make.
“Why six bands instead of one percentage?”A percentage invites false precision. The band carries the claim; the score carries the measure; the evidence line carries the limit.
“What happens when it is wrong?”It can be wrong inside a band, and the interface says so. When the input is insufficient, it returns unknown rather than a low score.
“Why is the interface French if the model may not be?”The interface is translated; model coverage is gated per language (D13, D23). The unsupported-language state never implies coverage.
“Why not show a score for the whole page?”An average of reviews would invent a claim the model cannot support. The panel counts reviews; it does not grade the product.
“What do you store?”History is off by default. When enabled: host, time, adapter, status, result, model version — never review text, review keys, or the URL.

A.7 String QA

  • One source. The copy deck is the only place a normative string is edited. Figma, code, and this appendix follow it.
  • Both languages together. A key is not done until English and French exist. No placeholder English in a French frame.
  • No concatenation. Build sentences whole; do not stitch “Band” + number + “of 6” in code, which breaks French word order and pluralisation.
  • No truncation. Test the longest French string at 320 px; the disclaimer and the refresh notice are the stress cases.
  • Numbers and spaces. English “31%”, French “31 %” with a no-break space; scores have no percent sign and no decimal.
  • Punctuation. French uses « guillemets » and a narrow no-break space before : ; ! ?. English uses curly quotes and an em dash without surrounding spaces.
  • Pluralisation. “1 review / 3 reviews”; “1 avis / 3 avis”. Zero takes the plural in both languages.
  • Forbidden words. No “AI”, “fake”, “bot”, “verified”, “confidence”, “accuracy”, “probability”, or “page score” in user-facing copy. The model is described by what it estimates, not by what it is.
