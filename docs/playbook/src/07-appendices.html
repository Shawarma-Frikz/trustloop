Timeout hierarchy

LayerBudgetOn expiry
Extension → backend6,000 msAbort the request; show the timeout state; offer manual retry.
Backend → scoring4,000 msAbort the call; mark every review in the batch UNKNOWN / SCORING_TIMEOUT.
Scoring computation3,000 msReturn no partial score. The backend still owns the user-facing state.
Why the order matters

The inner timeout must fire first so the backend can still answer with a well-formed batch. If the extension aborts first, the user loses every key in the batch, including reviews that would have succeeded. The outer budget exists for transport stalls, not for slow inference.

Contract event, not a silent addition

UNAUTHORIZED and FORBIDDEN arrive with accounts in Phase 4. Contract v1 is frozen, so publish a contract release before shipping them — either contracts/v2/ for the account surface while the analysis schemas stay v1, or promote the whole surface together. Update the enum-equality tests and the decision register. Never append codes to a frozen schema and hope the consumers ignore them.

Response shape by status

StatusEngineScoreRequired fields
SCOREDMODEL0–100p_organic, trustScore, verdict, evidenceQuality, up to five signals, modelVersion, engine.
SIGNALS_ONLYSIGNALS_ONLYNoneValidated signals, engine, status. No trustScore, no verdict, no numeric contribution, no modelVersion.
UNKNOWNMODELNonereasonCode, engine, status; optional evidenceQuality. Service-produced results may carry modelVersion.

Validation rules. The backend rejects any service response that has a score outside 0–100, a band outside the six fixed ranges, a signal count above five, an unknown engine or status, a score on an UNKNOWN, or a model version that does not match the expected pattern and value. A rejected response becomes SCORING_UNAVAILABLE for the whole batch. The extension applies the same shape checks before rendering and falls back to the unavailable state on violation.

Forbidden in any language

“Fake,” “fraud,” “bot account,” “spam account,” “verified,” “trustworthy reviewer,” “proven,” “AI-detected,” and any phrasing that turns a text-pattern estimate into a claim about a person, a purchase, or the truth of a review. A named pattern may be described only with its observable definition and its limits. The same rule binds the README, the report, the demo script, and the defense slides — not only the panel.

Appendix E — Signal taxonomy

Seven named patterns. Direction is preserved; only the top five by absolute contribution are returned. A signal says that a pattern contributed to the model output — never that it proves deception, intent, or identity.

CodeEnglish summary templateDirectionDefinition and limits
REPEATED_PHRASINGRepeats the same phrasingManipulativeRepeated n-grams or sentence templates within one review. Repetition also occurs in authentic reviews.
CROSS_REVIEW_DUPLICATEShares wording with other visible reviewsManipulativeServer-derived batch duplicate flag. Only within the request (maximum 10 reviews), not the whole page. Shared wording can be a template or a translation.
GENERIC_WORDINGUses generic wording with few concrete detailsManipulativeLow specificity and high generic-lexicon density. Domain-dependent; not proof of intent.
PROMOTIONAL_TONEReads like promotional copyManipulativePromotional lexicon and call-to-action patterns. Advertising and deception are not the same thing.
EXTREME_CLAIMSMakes extreme or unverifiable claimsManipulativeSuperlatives and absolute assertions. Stylistic; many genuine reviews do this.
CONCRETE_DETAILIncludes a concrete usage detailOrganicSpecificity features such as use case, duration, comparison. Detail can be fabricated.
LEXICAL_VARIETYShows varied, natural wordingOrganicType-token ratio and related diversity features. Unstable on short texts; model-dependent, so not used in SIGNALS_ONLY.

Rules for rendering a signal

  • Never quote the review. No raw text, names, URLs, product identifiers, or personal details in a summary or a tooltip.
  • Direction stays visible. A manipulative signal and an organic signal must not look identical. Use label, icon, and text — not colour alone.
  • Contributions are conditional. Show a number only when the explainer passes the stability and determinism checks. Otherwise the signal is qualitative and carries no percentage.
  • The returned subset is not renormalized. Contributions are normalized across the full feature set, then the top five are shown with their original values.
  • SIGNALS_ONLY uses observable patterns only. Cross-review duplication and repeated phrasing may be served without a model. Lexical variety and any model-dependent attribution may not.

Appendix F — CI blueprint

Five workflows, path-filtered so a documentation change does not rebuild a model. The required checks below are the ones enforced by the branch ruleset; everything else is advisory.

WorkflowTriggers on pathsRequired outcome
backend.ymlbackend/**, contracts/**Compiles on the approved JDK; unit and MockMvc tests pass; contract fixtures deserialize and rejected shapes stay rejected.
scoring.ymlscoring-service/**, contracts/**Lint, unit tests, service contract tests, and an artifact smoke test with a tiny fixture model. No full training in CI.
extension.ymlextension/**, contracts/**Lint, DOM-fixture tests, production build, packaged ZIP uploaded as an artifact.
contract.ymlcontracts/** and all component pathsEvery example validates against its schema; every component consumes the same fixtures; status, engine, verdict, and reason-code enumerations match.
docs.ymldocs/**, README.mdRelative links resolve; no placeholder credentials; copy-deck keys match across EN and FR.

Contract workflow shape

YAML · .github/workflows/contract.yml
name: contract
on:
  pull_request:
    paths:
      - "contracts/**"
      - "backend/**"
      - "scoring-service/**"
      - "extension/**"
  schedule:
    - cron: "0 2 * * *"

permissions:
  contents: read

jobs:
  validate:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: actions/setup-python@v5
        with:
          python-version: "3.12"
      - name: Install validator
        run: pip install jsonschema==4.23.0
      - name: Validate examples and enum parity
        working-directory: contracts/v1
        run: python validate.py
      - name: Contract tests
        env:
          TRUSTLOOP_CONTRACT_DIR: ${{ github.workspace }}/contracts/v1
        run: |
          ./backend/gradlew -p backend contractTest
          python -m pytest scoring-service/tests/contract
          npm --prefix extension run test:contract

Parity checks worth writing once. Assert that the Java enum, the Python enum, and the TypeScript union contain exactly the same reason codes and verdict codes; assert that every fixture round-trips through all three DTOs; assert that a service response with a score on an UNKNOWN is rejected by the backend and by the extension. These three tests catch almost every drift that matters.

Do not train in CI

Training is a deliberate, recorded act with a fixed seed, a dataset manifest, and a model card. CI validates artifacts and interface shape. A pipeline that silently retrains produces a model nobody evaluated — and a model version that no longer matches the reported gate.

Order

Schema and fixtures first, consumers second, documentation third — unless the change alters score meaning, in which case the decision register and the copy deck move with the schema. A consumer merged ahead of the contract is a broken main branch by definition.

Destructive commands

git reset --hard, git clean -fd, and git push --force are not part of a normal day. If one is genuinely required, confirm there is nothing uncommitted, confirm nobody else is on the branch, and prefer --force-with-lease on a feature branch — never on main.

Appendix H — Glossary

TermMeaning in this project
OrganicReview text whose patterns are consistent with a spontaneous review. Not a claim that the review is true or verified.
ManipulatedReview text whose patterns are consistent with manipulation. Not proof of intent or reviewer identity.
p_organicCalibrated probability that the text is organic, on the evaluation distribution — not a real-world prevalence.
Trust scoreInteger 0–100 equal to 100 × p_organic, rounded half up. A text-pattern estimate.
Verdict bandOne of six fixed score ranges. The name describes the model output, not the reviewer.
Evidence qualityLOW or ADEQUATE input sufficiency. Not model confidence, and never shown as a percentage.
SignalA named text pattern that contributed to the output, with direction and an optional validated contribution.
ContributionSigned, normalized attribution for a signal; shown only when the explainer is stable and validated.
EngineMODEL or SIGNALS_ONLY; identifies what produced the result.
StatusSCORED, SIGNALS_ONLY, or UNKNOWN.
UnknownNo estimate can be produced; carries a reason code and never a numeric score.
AdapterPage-specific extraction module with a manifest, selectors, supported-page description, and fixtures.
Eligible reviewA review card rendered and not hidden inside the adapter's container, with extractable text.
BatchUp to 10 reviews sent in one analyze request.
Session dedup keySalted page-session identifier used to avoid resubmission. Held in memory; never persisted.
CalibrationMapping from raw model output to p_organic; evaluated with ECE, Brier score, and reliability curves.
Model versiontl-text-<major>.<minor>.<patch>; carried by MODEL results and by service-produced unknowns.
Signal-only modeDescriptive, validated patterns without a score, probability, or numeric contribution.

TRUSTLOOP
Build the honest version.

A narrow system that tells the truth about what it measured is worth more than a broad one that guesses. When the schedule tightens, cut adapters, accounts, or deployment — never the test, the unknown state, or the meaning of the score.
